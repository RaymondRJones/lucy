# I-485 Full Packet for a Simple Marriage-Based Adjustment Case
Scope and assumptions
## Overview
This outline is for the standard immediate-relative marriage case: a U.S.-citizen petitioner, a spouse physically present in the United States after inspection and admission or parole, no children, no prior marriages, and no criminal, removal, J-1, or similar complications. According to U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, a spouse of a U.S. citizen is an immediate relative, visa numbers are immediately available in that category, and Form I-485 can be filed concurrently with Form I-130 or after the I-130 is filed while it remains pending.

If any non-simple issue exists — entry without inspection, prior removal, fraud or misrepresentation, J-1 two-year home residence issues, arrests or convictions, or other inadmissibility problems — the packet changes materially because USCIS adds extra required records or forms in those situations. Since you said “simple case,” the report below assumes none of those extra issues apply.
Filing posture

Because your requirements say the I-130 is already pending, the clean happy-path packet is usually the I-485 package itself: Form I-485, Form I-864, Form I-765, Form I-131, and Form I-693, with a copy of the I-130 receipt notice included in the I-485 section and the receipt number called out in the cover letter. When the I-130 is already pending, you do not refile Form I-130 or Form I-130A just to file the I-485.

If you were filing everything concurrently instead, USCIS says the I-130 and I-485 must be filed together on paper; if you choose to file the I-130 online first, you then wait for the receipt notice and include that receipt in the later I-485 packet. USCIS also notes that Form I-485 itself is not generally accepted online for this family-based packet.
Front of the packet
## Section Details
A cover letter is not a statutory requirement, but it is very helpful, and USCIS specifically tells filers to mark both the envelope and the cover letter with the type of submission and the form number. For your case, the cover letter should identify the petitioner and beneficiary, state the filing basis, say that this is an original marriage-based adjustment filing, mention the pending I-130 receipt number, and index every enclosed form and exhibit.

As of USCIS’s current indexed fee schedule, the paper fees that matter for this packet are: Form I-485 at $1,440 for most adult applicants filing with USCIS; Form I-765 under the pending-adjustment category at $260 if tied to a fee-paid I-485 filed on or after April 1, 2024; Form I-131 advance parole based on a pending I-485 at $630 on paper; and, only if you are still filing the petition concurrently, Form I-130 at $675 on paper or $625 online if filed separately first. Form I-864 has a $0 filing fee, and Form G-1450 has no extra fee. Because USCIS continues to update fee rules and payment guidance, the fee calculator is the safest final check right before mailing.

If you pay by card, use Form G-1450 and place it on top of the filing. USCIS warns that if you submit multiple forms together, each filing fee should be paid separately; a single combined payment can cause the entire package to be rejected. If you want lockbox acceptance by text and email, add optional Form G-1145 and clip it to the first page.
Section-by-section contents

I-485 section. The official immediate-relative checklist is the backbone here: signed Form I-485; two passport-style photos; a government-issued photo ID copy; a copy of the birth certificate with a full certified English translation if needed; inspection, admission, or parole evidence for the most recent entry; documentation of the immigrant category, such as the pending I-130 receipt notice; Form I-864 if required; and Form I-693 submitted in the same package. In a typical spousal adjustment case, the admission evidence is the passport biographic page, the visa page if applicable, the admission or parole stamp if available, and the Form I-94 printout from U.S. Customs and Border Protection for the most recent arrival. USCIS’s immediate-relative checklist is simpler than the family-preference checklist because it does not separately list proof of continuously maintained lawful status as an initial-evidence item for immediate relatives.

Marriage certificate and bona fide marriage evidence. This is the place to distinguish between what is strictly required and what is strategically smart. USCIS’s spouse-petition checklist for Form I-130 requires the marriage certificate and bona fide marriage evidence such as joint property, a joint lease, combined finances, children’s birth certificates if any, affidavits from people with personal knowledge, and other relevant documentation showing an ongoing marital union. Because your I-130 is already pending, the I-485 checklist itself focuses on the I-130 receipt notice rather than re-listing the full marriage-evidence package; still, in a real-world packet, including a copy of the marriage certificate and a compact set of updated bona fide marriage evidence is sensible because it keeps the I-485 packet self-contained and easier to review. Lead with the strongest formally joint evidence — joint bank statements and the lease — and then use utility bills and photos together as supplemental proof of shared residence and an ongoing relationship. Since this is a first marriage, you skip prior-marriage termination documents.

I-864 section. Most immediate-relative and family-based adjustment applicants must submit Form I-864. In the happy-path version of your packet, that section should include the signed Form I-864, the sponsor’s most recent federal tax return or tax transcript, every related W-2 and 1099 for that most recent year if you submit the return rather than a transcript, and any schedules that explain the income. USCIS also says pay stubs and an employer letter are useful current-income evidence, especially where you want to show that the sponsor’s present income clearly qualifies. For most family-based cases, the sponsor must show household income at or above 125 percent of the poverty guideline for the household size. If the petitioner has no children, no other dependents, and no prior I-864 obligations, the common household size in a simple spousal case is 2, but you should still confirm that carefully using the household-size rules. The currently indexed USCIS I-864P page lists the 125 percent threshold for a 2-person household in the lower 48 and similar jurisdictions at $26,437. If the petitioner cannot qualify alone, then the packet is no longer a simple case and you may need a joint sponsor and possibly Form I-864A.

I-765 section. If you file Form I-765 with the adjustment packet or while the I-485 is pending under the pending-adjustment category, include the signed Form I-765, two passport-style photos, a copy of the I-94, and proof of identity such as the passport biographic page or other government photo ID. If the applicant previously had an EAD, include a copy of the last EAD as well. If you file the I-765 later instead of in the original packet, include the I-485 receipt notice as proof of the pending adjustment case.

I-131 section. For advance parole based on a pending I-485, use the pending-adjustment option in Part 1, Item 5.A. USCIS says that if you file Form I-131 concurrently with Form I-485, you do not need to provide the I-485 receipt number there; if you file Form I-131 later, include the I-485 receipt notice. USCIS’s evidence list for an advance parole document for someone inside the United States calls for a photo ID copy, two passport-style photos, a document showing current status in the United States, and, for adjustment applicants, proof that Form I-485 was filed. In a simple marriage packet, the passport copy and the I-94 usually cover the identity and current-status pieces. USCIS also warns that leaving the United States without advance parole generally causes a pending I-485 to be treated as abandoned unless the person falls within certain H, L, K, or V exceptions, and advance parole itself is not a guarantee of admission on return.

I-130 and I-130A section if you are actually filing concurrently. If the I-130 is already pending, skip this section. If you are still filing the petition concurrently, include the signed I-130, the signed I-130A for the spouse beneficiary, proof of the petitioner’s U.S. citizenship, the marriage certificate, and the bona fide marriage evidence. USCIS says the spouse beneficiary must complete and sign Form I-130A when the I-130 is filed for a spouse, although a spouse who resides abroad does not sign it.

Medical section. This is now non-negotiable in the adjustment packet. USCIS requires Form I-693 to be submitted with Form I-485, and the civil surgeon must hand it back to you in a sealed envelope that you must not open. USCIS will not accept it if the envelope is open or altered. Civil-surgeon exam fees are not regulated by USCIS and vary by doctor. One important current nuance is that, under USCIS’s June 11, 2025 guidance, an I-693 signed on or after Nov. 1, 2023 is valid only while the application it was filed with remains pending; if that I-485 is denied or withdrawn and you file again later, you need a new I-693.
Recommended assembly order

USCIS does not publish one rigid family-based “master order” for every multi-form marriage packet, but it does tell filers to place payment first, then G-1145, then the forms and supporting documentation, and its adjustment policy manual shows the officer’s core I-485 record beginning with photos, the I-485, birth evidence, admission or parole evidence, the affidavit of support, and the medical exam. Based on that workflow, the cleanest order for your packet is the following.
# Core Sections
    Cover letter with the filing basis, petitioner and beneficiary identifiers, and the pending I-130 receipt number.
    Separate Form G-1450 payment sheets for each paid form.
    Form G-1145.
    I-485 section with photos, signed I-485, passport biographic page, visa page if applicable, admission or parole stamp if available, I-94, birth certificate with translation if needed, the I-130 receipt notice, the marriage certificate copy, and a compact update set of bona fide marriage evidence.
    I-864 section with financial support evidence.
    I-693 sealed envelope.
    I-765 section.
    I-131 section.
    I-130/I-130A section only if concurrent, otherwise omit it entirely.

That sequence is not the only possible workable order, but it aligns with USCIS lockbox guidance and with the policy manual’s core adjustment-file order, while also keeping the green-card application itself ahead of the ancillary work and travel forms.

For physical assembly, follow USCIS’s mailroom rules more than internet folklore. Use single-sided 8½ x 11 pages, keep everything easy to disassemble, avoid binders or folders, avoid heavy-duty staples, and do not send albums, scrapbooks, thumb drives, or other digital media. Light clips and plain letter-size divider sheets are sensible practical choices. If you include passport photos, lightly print the applicant’s name and A-Number, if any, on the back.

When you mail the packet, use the Form I-485 direct-filing address for the eligibility category and residence. If you include Forms I-765 and I-131 with the I-485, those related forms can go in the same envelope to the I-485 filing location. Keep a complete copy of everything you mail, along with tracking and copies of your payment forms.
Final checklist and watch-outs

Before sealing the envelope, confirm that every form is hand-signed; all pages are the current edition and all pages within each form are from the same edition; each paid form has its own separate payment authorization; all non-English documents have full certified translations; everything except the sealed I-693 and the original photo prints is a copy rather than an original unless USCIS specifically asks for the original; and the packet uses legible single-sided pages with consistent names, dates of birth, and A-Numbers across all forms. Then recheck the fee calculator and the direct filing address one last time, because USCIS may reject packets for wrong fees, wrong editions, unsigned forms, or incorrect filing locations.
